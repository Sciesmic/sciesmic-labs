    Model notes

    Each 6 hours the storm's wind moves 35% of the way toward a target set by three limits: the ocean's heat (warmer water allows stronger winds), wind shear (more shear lowers the target), and warm-layer depth (a shallow layer lets storm-stirred cold water cut the target after about 12–24 hours). A small random wobble of ±4 mph is added each step, so repeat runs differ slightly. Water over 26.5 °C is commonly used as the lowest temperature that supports tropical storms. Categories use the Saffir-Simpson wind scale. Numbers are for learning and are not predictions.
