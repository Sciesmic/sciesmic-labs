Earthquakes and Structures model notes

Version 1.0.0. An original educational model for grades 6–8. Component values are illustrative: they do not specify real buildings or predict their safety. All quantities use SI internally.

Motion and forces

Each floor has one horizontal displacement x relative to the ground. Each story has a spring and viscous damper. A two-bay row becomes one story spring; the simulation does not resolve stresses in individual beams or columns. For floor i:

mᵢ ẍᵢ = Fᵢ₊₁ − Fᵢ + Cᵢ₊₁ − Cᵢ − mᵢ a_ground(t)

The top has no force from a story above. Story deformation δᵢ = xᵢ − xᵢ₋₁. Elastic force Fᵢ = kᵢ(δᵢ − pᵢ), where p is plastic deformation. A one-dimensional plastic return step updates p when the force exceeds yield capacity plus isotropic hardening (hardening modulus = 0.03k). This creates permanent deformation and hysteretic energy dissipation. Unloading stiffness remains k; this model has no progressive fracture. Cᵢ = cᵢ(vᵢ − vᵢ₋₁). Integration is semi-implicit Euler at 0.001 s; samples are saved each 0.05 s. Numerical convergence is checked against smaller time steps.

Each bay needs a frame (beam plus two columns) and floor panel. Light/heavy panels add 3000/9000 kg and cost 2/3 points. Diagonal/X braces multiply bay stiffness by 1.8/2.5 and cost 3/5 points. Reinforced connections multiply stiffness by 1.15 and yield displacement by 1.5; cost 2 points. These represent configured assemblies, not a universal ranking of materials. Effective bay damping c = 2ζ√(k(m_frame + 3000)), with ζ = 0.025 or 0.18 with a damper (4 points); story c is the sum. The coefficient is a modeling parameter, not a statement that every vibration mode has this damping ratio.

A fixed foundation costs 4 points. An isolated foundation costs 12 points and adds a moving base mass equal to 12% of total floor mass. Isolation stiffness = total floor mass × (2π × 0.45 Hz)² and damping = 2 × 0.22 × √(stiffness × total floor mass). It is linear elastic with a 0.6 m travel limit. Isolator mass participates in the full system; 0.45 Hz is a parameter used to set stiffness, not the exact frequency of every coupled system.

Earthquake scenarios versus shake-table inputs

Earthquake magnitude is a source property. Local motion also depends on distance and geology. To make these relationships explorable, the lab uses this transparent teaching approximation, not an empirical ground-motion equation:

PGA / g = 0.035 × 10^[0.28(Mw − 5)] × [30 / √(R² + 10²)]^0.85 × S

R is source distance in km (5–100); S = 1 for rock or 1.5 for soft soil; Mw = 4–8. These constants set a useful classroom range and are not a calibrated site forecast. Duration = 5 + 2(Mw − 4) seconds. A seed sets five phases in a multi-frequency input. Relative frequencies are 0.45, 0.7, 1, 1.45, 2.1, with amplitudes 0.12, 0.2, 0.36, 0.2, 0.12. Reference frequency is 1.7 Hz for rock and 0.75 Hz for soft soil. The signal has a sin² envelope and is normalized to the target PGA using a 0.001 s grid. Soil changes only the input; this is not a soil–structure interaction model.

Controlled shake-table mode uses a single frequency (0.3–5 Hz), amplitude (0–0.8 g), and duration (3–20 s) with the same envelope. All modes add 8 s of unforced motion. Shake-table repeats are deterministic; earthquake phases vary only with the seed. Reset starts with an undamaged design. There is no sensor noise or random failure probability.

Evidence and limits

Story drift = 100 × δ / 3 m. Roof displacement is relative to ground; roof acceleration is absolute (relative acceleration plus ground acceleration). Major model damage means yielding occurred and peak drift exceeded 2.5 times that story’s equivalent yield drift. Yielded means plastic deformation occurred. An 8% story-drift or 60 cm isolation-travel exceedance stops the solver. Neither a damage label nor a stop is a certified collapse prediction. Residual roof displacement is the value at the end of the record, not guaranteed permanent rest.

Visible sway is enlarged ×35 and capped for display at 95 SVG units; numeric data are not capped. The scene does not animate absolute ground displacement because acceleration is the prescribed input. No torsion, vertical motion, liquefaction, buckling, fracture, individual member stresses, or real construction codes are simulated. There is no universal earthquake-proof design.

Challenge: at least 3 stories, at most 90 fictional design points, no yielding, peak drift ≤1.5%, and no model-limit stop in each of three fixed scenarios. Comparison of saved trials is evidence for redesign within this model.

Sources

USGS magnitude and intensity; USGS monitoring building response and isolation; NIST earthquake input energy and damping. These support the physical concepts; the illustrative model constants are Sciesmic choices.

Resumen en español

Modelo educativo de masas por piso, resortes elastoplásticos y amortiguadores viscosos. Cada entrepiso mide 3 m. La deriva es el desplazamiento relativo entre pisos dividido entre su altura y multiplicado por 100. El movimiento lateral está ampliado ×35 solo en el dibujo. Los valores numéricos no se amplían.

La magnitud, distancia y suelo generan un registro sintético mediante una aproximación didáctica; no es una ecuación empírica ni un pronóstico local. La mesa vibratoria permite controlar amplitud, frecuencia y duración. La semilla controla las fases del registro; repetir diseño, semilla y condiciones reproduce resultados. Reiniciar elimina el daño de la prueba anterior y conserva la evidencia registrada.

Los materiales y capacidades son ilustrativos. Las dos crujías se reducen a un resorte por piso; no se calculan esfuerzos de cada elemento. La fluencia produce deformación permanente. El daño mayor se marca por encima de 2.5 veces la deriva de fluencia después de que ocurre fluencia. El modelo se detiene al superar 8% de deriva o 60 cm de recorrido de aislamiento; esto no predice colapso real. No simula torsión, movimiento vertical, licuefacción ni fractura.
